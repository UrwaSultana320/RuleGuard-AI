The applicant’s problem

An international student applying for a Master’s program finds English-language rules scattered across official documents. One may accept a Medium of Instruction (MOI) certificate while another requires IELTS or TOEFL.

The student needs to understand which conditions apply and what needs university confirmation before making a costly application mistake.

The distinctive idea

Compare requirements across documents in the applicant’s context and explain every finding with evidence. Distinguish a potential conflict from rules for different programs, intakes, or applicant categories.

English-language proficiency is the first demonstration scenario. MOI versus IELTS/TOEFL is an example drawn from document evidence, not hardcoded product logic. The AI explains uncertainty instead of automatically choosing which document is correct.

The user journey

Applicant context includes degree level, program, intake, and category. PDF support is limited to files containing readable text.

Key features within this journey

Context-aware comparison

Check whether requirements apply to the same program, intake, and applicant category. Success requires recognizing differences in any of these contexts and not incorrectly flagging them as contradictions.

Verifiable evidence

Show exact supporting text from both documents and source references, including page numbers for PDFs.

Plain-language reasoning

Explain agreements, possible conflicts, differing conditions, and requirements that cannot be verified. Highlight uncertainty clearly.

A useful next step

Suggest a specific clarification question the applicant can send to the university.

One complete, reliable comparison journey demonstrated with English-language requirements. The three validation cases below stay within this scenario. Expand each boundary to review what belongs in this proof of concept.

Three testable demonstration cases

1 · Genuine contradiction

Input: Both documents explicitly cover the same program, intake, and applicant category. One permits MOI instead of a language test; the other explicitly requires IELTS/TOEFL and rejects MOI as a substitute, with no stated exception reconciling them.

Pass: Flag a potential contradiction, cite both incompatible statements and their shared context, explain the conflict, and suggest a clarification question without choosing the authoritative document.

2 · Compatible requirements in different contexts

Input: Different English-language requirements explicitly cover different programs, intakes, or applicant categories.

Pass: Identify and cite the different applicable conditions, explain which applies to the applicant where supported, and do not flag a contradiction. Validate variations covering each dimension: program, intake, and applicant category.

3 · Missing or ambiguous evidence

Input: A readable document omits MOI eligibility or does not clearly identify the applicable program, intake, or applicant category.

Pass: Identify what cannot be verified, cite available relevant evidence, explain the gap or ambiguity, and suggest a targeted clarification question. Do not invent evidence or assert an unsupported contradiction or agreement.

RuleGuard AI

Context-aware comparison of requirements across documents, with evidence and transparent uncertainty. English-language proficiency for international Master's applicants is the first hackathon demonstration scenario.

The Real-World Problem

International applicants must interpret requirements scattered across official websites, PDFs, and admission guidelines. Apparent conflicts and unclear exceptions can lead to costly application mistakes. They need to know what applies to them and what requires official confirmation.

The Unique Kernel

Context-aware comparison of requirements across documents, with evidence-based explanations and transparency when the AI is uncertain. The system compares the requirements and applicable conditions found in the supplied documents. English-language proficiency is the first demonstration scenario; MOI versus IELTS/TOEFL is an example, not hardcoded product logic.

Who It's For

An international student applying for a Master's program who needs to understand English-language proficiency requirements, especially whether a Medium of Instruction (MOI) certificate is accepted or IELTS/TOEFL is required.

The Core Loop

The applicant uploads two relevant documents and provides basic context such as degree level, intended program, intake, and applicant category. RuleGuard AI extracts and compares requirements in that context, then presents a simple visual report with evidence and questions the applicant can send to the university.

Preliminary AI findings suggest agreements, potential contradictions, different applicable conditions, and requirements that cannot be verified. Users inspect cited evidence and the full extracted source text, then accept, reject, or mark each suggestion as uncertain. These review choices do not establish accuracy, applicant eligibility, or complete requirement coverage. The applicant leaves with clearer requirements and knows what needs official confirmation.
